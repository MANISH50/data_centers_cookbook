    How Netflix streams: the whole tour as text

    What happens between pressing Play and the first frame, where each step physically runs, and how Netflix’s data-center strategy changed from 2007 to 2025. 15 stops.

    01What happens when you press Play?

    What happens. In a second or two, your TV talks to the cloud, gets permission, and starts pulling video from a box that is probably inside your own internet provider. Fly through it stop by stop: scroll, swipe, drag, use the arrow keys, or press Next. Watch the moving dots: each colour is a different kind of traffic, named in the legend.

    Data-center role📍 Device

    Five kinds of physical place are involved: your device, your ISP’s site, internet exchanges, AWS regions, and Netflix’s Open Connect network linking them.

    Sources: [1]

    02You press Play

    What happens. One press starts two separate journeys. The control plane (red) runs in AWS: who you are, what you may watch, and where to fetch it from. The data plane (teal) is the video itself, served by Netflix’s own Open Connect network.

    Data-center role📍 AWS region

    Since early 2016 Netflix has run no data centers of its own for streaming. Compute lives in AWS regions; video lives on Netflix-built appliances inside ISPs and exchanges.

    Sources: [3] [1]

    03Inside the app

    What happens. Before asking for anything, the app knows what your device can do: resolution, HDR, and codecs such as AV1, HEVC or AVC. Video is protected by DRM. The app will need a licence for the device’s DRM system (Widevine, PlayReady or FairPlay). Its adaptive-bitrate (ABR) player keeps a buffer of video and picks a quality to match your connection.

    Data-center role📍 Device

    The device is the outermost edge. It pulls its menus, artwork and settings from AWS. No video has moved yet.

    Sources: [1]

    04The request leaves home

    What happens. Your play request travels router → ISP → the internet → an AWS region. These are small API calls: a few kilobytes, not video. DNS decides which AWS region answers you.

    Data-center role📍 AWS region

    Netflix runs active-active across AWS regions: members are routed by DNS to a nearby region, and any healthy region can serve any member.

    Sources: [8]

    05Zuul, the edge gateway

    What happens. Every request first meets Zuul, Netflix’s edge gateway. It routes requests to the right service, applies security checks, and can shed less important load to protect the services behind it. It can also send traffic to another region. Try it: fail a region below.

    Data-center role📍 AWS region

    Zuul fronts all of Netflix’s edge services in each region. In a region-wide outage, tools override DNS and move members to a healthy region.

    Sources: [9] [8] [10]

    06Microservices

    What happens. Behind Zuul, many small services each do one job: entitlement, profiles, playback, licensing, recommendations, billing. Data lives in EVCache (a fast cache), Cassandra (a database) and Keystone, the Kafka-based event pipeline. Titus runs the containers, Spinnaker ships new code, and Chaos Monkey randomly switches servers off to prove the system survives.

    Data-center role📍 AWS region

    Services spread across availability zones, and data is copied between regions, so losing one building or one region does not lose member data.

    Sources: [3] [8] [11]

    07Steering

    What happens. The playback service checks your account and licensing, then works out which files you need. The steering service picks healthy, nearby Open Connect Appliances (OCAs) that hold those files. Your device gets back a manifest, a ranked list of OCA URLs and a licence.

    Data-center role📍 AWS region

    The decision runs in AWS, using data from the edge: every OCA reports its health, the routes it has learned and the files it stores to the control plane in AWS.

    Sources: [1] [5]

    08Before you pressed Play: encoding

    What happens. The studio master is split into chunks and shots, and encoded in parallel in the cloud on Cosmos, Netflix’s media platform. Per-title encoding (2015) gave each title its own settings; per-shot encoding (2018) tunes every shot, judged by the VMAF quality score. The result is a ladder of qualities in AV1, HEVC and AVC. AV1 film grain synthesis re-creates grain on your device instead of sending it.

    Data-center role📍 AWS region

    Encoding is a burst workload on AWS EC2: it scales up for new releases and down when idle. Masters and finished files are stored in S3, the origin for the edge.

    Sources: [14] [12] [13] [16] [3] [6]

    09The overnight fill

    What happens. Netflix predicts what members will watch, and when. In off-peak “fill windows” it copies tomorrow’s popular titles to OCAs: the catalogue to storage OCAs at internet exchanges, the most popular titles to OCAs inside ISPs. OCAs can also fill from each other, saving backbone traffic.

    Data-center role📍 Netflix backbone

    Files move from the S3 origin in AWS over Netflix’s backbone to exchange sites, then out to ISP sites, mostly at night when networks are quiet.

    Sources: [6] [1] [7]

    10An OCA inside your ISP

    What happens. An Open Connect Appliance is a Netflix-built server, given free to qualifying ISPs. It runs FreeBSD and the NGINX web server on bare metal. It stores video files and serves them over HTTPS, and nothing else: no member data. Netflix lists its current storage appliance at around 200 Gbps and up to 120 TB.

    Data-center role📍 ISP site

    The ISP’s building becomes Netflix’s video data center. Netflix supplies the hardware; the ISP supplies space, power and connectivity, and saves upstream traffic.

    Sources: [2] [1]

    11Video flows

    What happens. The player fetches short segments from the top-ranked OCA. If your connection slows, ABR steps down the quality ladder; when it recovers, it steps back up. If an OCA fails, the player moves to the next URL on its list. Try both buttons.

    Data-center role📍 ISP site

    Open Connect serves 100% of Netflix’s video traffic, not AWS. Playback quality data flows back to AWS to improve steering.

    Sources: [5] [1]

    12Live

    What happens. Netflix’s first live show was in 2023: Chris Rock’s Selective Outrage. The venue feed is encoded in real time in the cloud. A custom Live Origin passes segments to Open Connect. Tyson vs. Paul in November 2024 peaked at 65 million concurrent streams.

    Data-center role📍 AWS region

    Live can’t be pre-filled. Encoding runs in AWS in real time; the Live Origin, on EC2, feeds top-tier Open Connect caches, which pass segments down as viewers ask for them.

    Sources: [17] [18]

    13Data-center evolution, 2007–2012

    What happens. 2007: streaming begins, from Netflix’s own data centers. 2008: a major database corruption stops DVD shipping for three days, and the move to the cloud begins. Services are rebuilt cloud-native on AWS. 2011: Chaos Monkey goes public. 2012: Open Connect is announced.

    Data-center role📍 AWS region

    A big relational database in one owned facility was a single point of failure. Netflix chose horizontally scalable cloud services over growing its own data centers.

    Sources: [3] [11] [19] [1]

    142015–2025

    What happens. 2015: per-title encoding. January 2016: the last data center is shut down, and Netflix reaches 190 countries. 2018: per-shot encoding. 2020: AV1 on Android. 2023: the first live show. 2024–25: live at scale.

    Data-center role📍 Netflix backbone

    The end state is hybrid: rented, elastic compute in AWS for logic and encoding, plus owned, purpose-built hardware at the network edge for the bytes.

    Sources: [12] [3] [4] [13] [15] [17] [18]

    15The whole system

    What happens. Brains in the cloud: AWS decides who you are, what you may watch and where to get it. Bytes at the edge: Open Connect boxes near you send the video. Prepared in advance: titles are encoded and pre-positioned overnight; only live is made on the fly.

    Data-center role📍 AWS region
